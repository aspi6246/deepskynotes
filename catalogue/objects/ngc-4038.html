Background

NGC 4038 and NGC 4039 are the Antennae Galaxies — a pair of colliding spirals in Corvus, catalogued together as Arp 244. Around 60 million light-years away (distance estimates range \(\sim\)45–65 Mly), the two disks are mid-merger: their cores are drawing together while tidal forces have flung out two long, faint streams of stars and gas that arc away to the north and south. Those streams — clearly seen on the DSS plate — give the pair its name. The collision has triggered a burst of star formation, and long-exposure images show the merged bodies studded with young blue clusters. The listed magnitudes are blue (B); no integrated visual magnitude is catalogued for the individual components. Visually the pair reads as two soft, merging glows (the brighter “comma” of NGC 4038 to the north, NGC 4039 hooking below it); the antennae themselves are a photographic feature, not an eyepiece one.
