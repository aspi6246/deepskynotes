Background

M 2 is one of the great globulars of the sky — ≈150,000 stars in a ball ≈37,000 light-years away and around 13 billion years old, among the most ancient objects the eyepiece will ever show. At magnitude 6.5 it is binocular-easy; moderate apertures resolve its edges into diamond dust around the fiercely compressed core, with a distinctive dark notch on the northeast rim for those who look carefully.
