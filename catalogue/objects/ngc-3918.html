Background

NGC 3918 is the “Blue Planetary” — one of the brightest and most easily identified planetary nebulae in the southern sky, ≈4,900 light-years away in Centaurus. Its vivid blue disk is close enough in apparent size (≈18”) and colour to the planet Uranus that John Herschel, who discovered it in 1834, coined the nickname. The high surface brightness makes it stand out even at low power as an obviously non-stellar, slightly out-of-focus “star”; higher magnification shows a small, uniformly bright, faintly elliptical disk with a hint of a fainter outer shell. A hot central star drives the rich emission that gives the nebula its striking colour.

My Observing Notes

44-cm (Club 17.5-inch f/5): Easy to spot in the 26mm as an elongated disk among the stars — blinking with an OIII filter makes it jump out of the field. It forms a triangle with two other bright stars. A hint of blue — or perhaps an illusion, since the OIII lends the field stars a blue hue. At 13mm (171×, 0.6° TFOV) + OIII the disk is clearly not uniform: structure within the nebula, perhaps layers of gas.(Friday, July 2026)
