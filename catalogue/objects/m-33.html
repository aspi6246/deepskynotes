Background

M 33 is the Triangulum Galaxy (NGC 598), a face-on SA(s)cd spiral and the third-largest member of the Local Group after the Andromeda Galaxy and the Milky Way, ≈2.7 million light-years away. Its total magnitude of 5.7 is spread over more than a degree of sky, giving a surface brightness of only ≈14 mag per square arcminute. It needs a low-power eyepiece and a dark, transparent sky, and from Wiruna it is harder still: it never rises more than ≈26° above the northern horizon. Larger apertures pick out the spiral arms and, ≈11' from the core, the giant H II region NGC 604.
